# Visual grammar

## Evidence scenes

1. Establish the full document, page, application, or chart.
2. Introduce a pointer, mask, connection, or state change.
3. Hold long enough to read the evidence.
4. Keep captions from covering the evidence.

Avoid continuous zoom on an already cropped screenshot. Avoid decorative stock images when the narration names a real feature, result, or interface.

## Conceptual scenes

Use small animations to express verbs:

- ingest: cards converge into a pipeline;
- parse: a scan line produces fields;
- retrieve: candidates move and reorder;
- bind: claim and evidence connect;
- synthesize: waveform grows by segment;
- synchronize: parallel tracks share markers;
- validate: gates turn from pending to pass;
- iterate: only affected nodes relight.

Animation must explain state or causality. Do not add movement only to prevent stillness.

## Layout

- Use one dominant idea per scene.
- Preserve 5–8% outer safe margins.
- Prefer two-level hierarchy: statement plus proof.
- Keep small UI readable at delivery resolution; use overlays instead of destructive crops.
- Use consistent caption placement and sufficient contrast.
- Check the contact sheet for repeated compositions and excessive visual sameness.
